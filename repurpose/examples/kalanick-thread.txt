Travis Kalanick ran a $70 billion company "the way somebody who thought he was going to starve next week would run it." His David Senra interview is the best founder masterclass this year. What stuck with me:

"The only constraint on our imagination is management capacity." Not money, not ideas. If you can solve problems faster than you create them, you can keep creating them. He calls the balance between the two the meta problem.

On the taxi industry Uber broke: "a government-condoned cartel that outlaws competition." A New York driver paid $40,000 a year to rent a car for 12-hour shifts, from a guy whose grandpa got the medallion free.

On the Didi war: "efficiency outstrips subsidy." Get bigger, get more efficient, and at some scale your edge beats any subsidy a rival can afford. That, he says, is why Lyft is smaller than Uber.

On fundraising: "I never get attached to a price. I get attached to a process." Gurley told him to take the first term sheet at $6B. He ran his process instead and closed at $17.5B pre two months later.

On management: "the fewest number of rules while staying out of chaos." At Uber, 23-year-olds launched whole cities with one rule: nothing launches until Travis says yes on the pricing call.
