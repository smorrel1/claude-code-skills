A New York taxi driver was paying $40,000 a year to rent a car for 12-hour shifts. The man he rented it from held a medallion his grandfather got for free. Travis Kalanick's summary: "For that privilege, he gets to be impoverished."

That story, from his recent interview with David Senra, explains Uber better than any growth-hacking retrospective. Kalanick wasn't fighting taxis. He was fighting what he calls "a government-condoned cartel that outlaws competition."

Two other ideas from the conversation earn a place on the office wall.

First: "The only constraint on our imagination is management capacity." He frames it as an inequality, problem-solving rate must stay at or above problem-creation rate, or you stop expanding until it does. Most founders track neither.

Second, on fundraising: "I never get attached to a price. I get attached to a process." When Bill Gurley pushed him to take the first term sheet at $6B, he ran a proper auction instead and closed at $17.5B pre two months later. The lesson isn't the number. It's that alternatives, not conviction, are what move a negotiation.

The whole interview runs two hours and repays all of them. One question to sit with: are you creating problems faster than you can solve them?
